## Context note

Section 17 of the Copyright Act, 1957 addresses who is the first owner of
copyright in a work. As a general principle under this Act, the author of a
work is its first owner of copyright.

However, there are exceptions: when a work is made by an author in the course
of employment under a contract of service or apprenticeship, the employer is
considered the first owner of copyright (subject to any agreement to the
contrary). This exception is commonly used by clients to argue that freelancers
working under certain contract structures do not own the copyright to their work.

Freelancers should be aware that whether their engagement is characterised as a
"contract of service" (employment) or a "contract for services" (independent
contractor) can affect who holds first copyright ownership.

**Important limitation:** This note is for contextual reference only.
The applicable characterisation depends on the specific contract terms and
facts. Whether Section 17 applies and in what manner is a matter for a
qualified lawyer. Do not use this reference to conclude that any particular
contract clause transfers or retains copyright.
